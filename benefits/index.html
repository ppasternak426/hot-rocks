Sauna benefits

Warm up by the stove, step out for fresh air and take your time by Lough Neagh. A visit here gives you a little space to unwind and let tired muscles rest.

What to enjoy in a sauna session

A calmer evening and better sleep

Settle onto a warm bench and let the pace of the day fall away. A quiet sauna gives you room to switch off and ease into the evening. Take that slower rhythm home as part of winding down for a better night’s sleep.

Study links
  • Relaxation, wellbeing and sleep · Global Sauna Survey (2019)

Warmth, circulation and blood pressure

Feel the warmth spread as more blood flows to your skin. Blood pressure can dip briefly as you cool down after the heat. Take a seat outside and enjoy a few quiet minutes while your body finds its rhythm again.

Study links
  • Blood pressure after a sauna session · Laukkanen et al. (2018)
  • Regular sauna and heart health · Debray et al. (2023)
  • Sauna habits and stroke · Kunutsor et al. (2018)

Fresh air and room to breathe

There’s something lovely about stepping out of the warmth and into fresh air by the lough. Let your breathing settle, look out over the water and head back inside when you’re ready.

Study links
  • Sauna and common colds · Ernst et al. (1990)
  • Sauna and pneumonia · Kunutsor et al. (2017)

Soothing warmth for aches and stiffness

After a long walk or a day at a desk, a warm bench is a welcome place to rest. Settle in and enjoy the soothing warmth on tired muscles and stiff joints.

Study links
  • Infrared sauna, joint pain and stiffness · Oosterveld et al. (2009)

Sauna routines and brain health

Make a little space in your week for a break from the usual hurry. A regular sauna visit gives you a familiar rhythm to look forward to, with time to clear your head, sit quietly or catch up with someone.

Study links
  • Sauna, dementia and Alzheimer’s · Laukkanen et al. (2017)
  • Sauna habits and dementia · Knekt et al. (2020)

The Hot Rocks Sauna Quiz

Question 1 of 3

How many sauna users said they slept better?

Think of 10 people who answered the sleep question in a survey of sauna users.

About 8 in 10

353 of the 423 people who answered the sleep question reported slightly or much better sleep after sauna use. They volunteered for the survey and rated their own sleep, so it tells us how this group felt.

  • Hussain et al., Global Sauna Survey (2019)

Question 2 of 3

Where did the blood pressure reading land?

The average top reading was 137 mmHg before a 30-minute study session. Guess the average immediately afterwards. A guess up to 2 mmHg above or below earns a point.

137 mmHg

130 mmHg

The top reading fell by 7 mmHg on average among 102 adults with at least one cardiovascular risk factor. That was an immediate change. In a separate eight-week trial of adults with stable coronary artery disease, researchers found no improvement in resting blood pressure.

  • Laukkanen et al., single-session study (2018)
  • Debray et al., eight-week randomised trial (2023)

Question 3 of 3

How big was the difference in the stroke study?

A Finnish study compared adults taking 4–7 saunas a week with adults going once a week. About how much lower was the estimated stroke rate in the more frequent group after researchers accounted for other risk factors?

About 60% lower

The adjusted relative estimate was 61% lower. The observed rates were 2.8 versus 8.1 strokes per 1,000 person-years. Researchers followed 1,628 adults for a median of nearly 15 years. They tracked existing habits, so the study could not show whether sauna use caused the difference.

  • Kunutsor et al., Finnish stroke study (2018)
